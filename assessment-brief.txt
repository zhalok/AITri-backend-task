Part 2: Product Spec
2.1 Background
Leadership teams hold a weekly meeting to check their numbers, surface problems and agree on next steps. This product gives each team one place to track its weekly numbers (Measurables), quarterly goals (Rocks), tasks (To-Dos) and problems (Issues), and to run that meeting. It is built around a loop that repeats every week:
* Measure: a handful of weekly Measurables show whether the business is healthy.
* Commit: quarterly Rocks and weekly To-Dos make clear who does what, and by when.
* Resolve: anything off track becomes an Issue, and the team solves it together in the meeting.
Keep the loop in mind and many of your modeling and layout decisions get easier.
Out of scope: sign-up (a seeded user switcher or simple login is enough), email, billing, integrations, Private To-Dos, Long-Term Issues, creating or editing teams, users, Measurables or Rocks (they come from the seed data), a meeting history page, and the dashed links in 2.2 (two of them are Bonus items in Part 3).
2.2 How It Fits Together
 How the parts of the app connect 

How the parts of the app connect
Solid links are what you build: an off-track Score becomes an Issue, solving an Issue produces To-Dos, and the Level 10 Meeting runs through all three. Dashed links show how Ninety connects the rest, so the domain makes sense; you don't build them. The last closed week means the full week before the current one.
These are the names used in Ninety's EOS edition. Ninety's newer edition calls them KPI, Weekly Team Meeting and RDR (Raise, Discuss, Resolve).
2.3 Core Journey
A team's week, from one user's view:
1. Sign in as a seeded user and pick one of your teams.
2. Before the meeting, enter the last closed week's scores, mark To-Dos done, and add Issues.
3. An Admin or Team Member starts the Level 10 Meeting and becomes the facilitator. Teammates join from their own browsers.
4. Scorecard (5 min): the team reviews the last closed week's scores and uses Make it an Issue on off-track ones.
5. IDS (60 min): the team works the Issues list in order, solves Issues, and adds To-Dos for follow-up.
6. Conclude (10 min): the team recaps the new To-Dos, attendees rate the meeting, and the facilitator ends it.
7. Attendees see the meeting's summary.
2.4 Users and Roles
Each user has one company-wide role and belongs to one or more teams.
Role

	Admin

	Team Member

	2.5 Requirements by Page
Two pages. The Scorecard and Issues sections are built once and reused on both. Seed data is the starting state. Actions are for Admins and Team Members; Observers see everything read-only. Computed values follow 2.6.
Team Page
A team switcher in the header lists the user's teams (every team for Admins). A team the user can't access returns an error, even by guessed ID.

	Title, owner, due date, done time and source Issue. Overdue ones flagged. Last closed week's completion rate.

	Rocks

	Meeting Page
* Layout: the agenda (Scorecard 5 min, IDS 60 min, Conclude 10 min), the section timer and attendees. Scorecard and IDS reuse the Team page sections with all their actions. Conclude shows the meeting's new To-Dos and ratings.
* Control: an Admin or Team Member starts the meeting and becomes the facilitator. One active meeting per team. Anyone who can read the team can join. Only the facilitator advances or ends it, at any time.
* Rating: in Conclude, each attending Admin or Team Member rates 1 to 10 and can change it until the end.
* Sync: everyone sees the same section and timer, and changes reach all attendees within about a second. The server owns the timer; an overrun section shows as over time and doesn't advance. Late joins, refreshes and reconnects (facilitator included) get the current state. No facilitator handoff needed.
* Summary: at the end, attendees see Measurable statuses for the last closed week, Issues solved and To-Dos created during the meeting, attendees and the rating. The API also returns any ended meeting's summary, including the 2 seeded ones.
2.6 Business Rules
These have exact answers and are checked against the seed data.
Time
* A week runs Monday 00:00:00 to Sunday 23:59:59 in the company time zone (America/Chicago). A quarter is a calendar quarter.
* Due dates are calendar dates in company time. A To-Do is overdue when it is not done and its due date is before today (the date of APP_NOW in company time).
* Round half up (12.5% → 13%, 8.25 → 8.3).
Rule
	Definition
	Edge cases
	Measurable status
	Compare the week's score with the goal: at least, at most, between, or exactly
	Boundaries are on track. A closed week with no score is off track. The current week with no score is pending.
	To-Do completion rate
	To-Dos done by the end of a week ÷ To-Dos due that week. Target is 90%.
	Exactly 90% meets target. Done after its due week counts as not done. Zero due means no rate.
	Rock status
	Complete if its seeded status is Complete. Off-track if its seeded status is Off-track, or if an unfinished Milestone is past due. Otherwise On-track.
	A Milestone due today is not past due. No Milestones means the seeded status alone.
	Rock progress
	Completed Milestones ÷ total Milestones, as a whole percent
	No Milestones: 0%, or 100% once Complete
	Issue order
	Priority 5 first, down to 1. Within a priority, oldest first.
	No priority comes last
	Meeting rating
	Average of submitted ratings, to one decimal
	Attendees who didn't rate are excluded. No ratings means no score.
	No duplicate Issues
	Make it an Issue on a Measurable and week that already has an unsolved linked Issue returns that Issue
	Holds when two people click at the same moment
	2.7 Technical Requirements
* Stack: Next.js with TypeScript, shadcn/ui and Tailwind. FastAPI. Postgres with migrations.
* Authorization: every role rule in 2.4 is enforced on the server. Hiding a button is not authorization.
* Data integrity: database constraints reject invalid data, not only the app.
* Time: store timestamps in UTC. Read "now" from APP_NOW, not the system clock, and default it to 2026-11-11T15:00:00Z (Wednesday, November 11, 2026, 9:00 AM Chicago), the seed's "now".
* Operations: docker compose up starts the whole stack with seed data loaded. The seed script is safe to rerun.
* API: a documented REST API serves everything in 2.5.
* Seed data: in seed.json, in the zip attached to the email. 1 company (America/Chicago), 3 teams, 9 users across all three roles (some on more than one team), Measurables with 13 weeks of scores, Q4 2026 Rocks and Milestones, To-Dos, Issues, and 2 ended meetings with their attendees and ratings. Some records sit minutes from a week boundary on purpose.
